We price by the day because no two pieces of Hill Country ground clear the same. The real question is how many days your land will take, and that depends on more than acreage.

Why acreage alone doesn't set the price

Density

A few scattered cedars across open pasture go fast. A solid wall of brush takes a lot longer per acre.

Mature vs. young cedar

Small cedar mulches quickly. Big, old trees with thick trunks take more time to grind down.

Rocky or steep ground

Limestone ledges, loose rock and slopes slow the machine down and call for more care.

Access and gates

Narrow gates, creek crossings and long drives to the work area all affect how the day goes.

Selective work around oaks

Working carefully around the oaks and shade trees you want to keep takes more time than clearing everything.

The finish you want

A rough opening is quicker than a clean, park-like finish down close to the ground.

Hill Country know-how

Oak wilt

Oak wilt kills a lot of live oaks and red oaks around here. The Texas A&M Forest Service and texasoakwilt.org recommend avoiding pruning or wounding oaks from February through June, when the sap beetles that spread the fungus are most active, and painting any fresh wound on an oak right away, any time of year. We work carefully around the oaks you're keeping, and if one gets nicked, it gets painted on the spot.

texasoakwilt.org · Texas A&M Forest Service

Golden-cheeked warbler

The golden-cheeked warbler is a federally endangered songbird that nests only in central Texas, in mature Ashe juniper and oak woodland. It's here roughly March through July. If your property has that kind of old, closed-canopy cedar and oak, clearing it may call for care around nesting season, and it may be worth checking U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service guidance before work starts. We're not biologists or lawyers, so for anything beyond a heads-up, go to the source.

U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service · Texas Parks & Wildlife guidelines

Common questions

Do you haul off debris?

No. Cedar and brush get mulched in place and left on the ground. There are no burn piles.

Can you work around my oaks?

Yes. You pick what stays, and we work around it.

How long does the mulch take to break down?

It depends on chip size, moisture and how much sun the ground gets. Extension services generally say surface wood-chip mulch thins out noticeably over about 1 to 2 years, while bigger chunks and wood buried in soil can take many years to fully decompose. In our dry Hill Country summers, plan on a few years before most of it is gone.
